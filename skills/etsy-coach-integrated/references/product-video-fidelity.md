# Source-locked gift video production

## Bind the actual brief
Record exact SKU/variant, current source images/artwork/logo, native pixels, verified dimensions, printed text, personalization fields, target duration, aspect ratio, active generation/editing method and current approval scope. Recheck tools and destination capabilities rather than inheriting old “no account” assumptions. Tutorial metadata, transcripts and a successful sample do not prove mastery of every feature.

Plan a short gift story with distinct useful beats, not fifteen seconds of the same slight movement. Use the requested emotional introduction, product identity, verified personalization/feature explanation and brand ending. Show generic adult sender/recipient through believable interaction when requested; a faceless crop must retain torsos, hands and relationship context without implying a real customer/testimonial.

For the emotional copy and visual expression gate, read [emotional-video-expression.md](emotional-video-expression.md). Technical fidelity alone is not creative acceptance; evaluate exact words, typography, scene and pacing together.

## Preserve product rather than regenerate lettering
When the current video workflow permits compositing, keep exact same-SKU print/art outside the motion model and use supported perspective and occlusion. Preserve shape, edge color, texture, contact shadow, accessories and cable continuously. A stable center-text ROI does not validate the perimeter. Do not generate a new book spine, inner pages, page block or cable variant merely to make a shot attractive.

Use physical support: a closed journal resting on the table while both people gently hold its lower edges can communicate giving/support without lifting or rocking the cover. Keep fingers and contact planes credible. If motion causes drifting edges, seams, changing wood grain, cord disappearance or grey rims, correct only a source-supported region and verify all frames. Avoid a frozen background rectangle that freezes moving clothing or hands.

A stationary editorial alternative is valid only when the active task accepts it. Label it static people/scene with animated information, not generated human motion. A previous request for motion remains a failure gate until explicitly changed. Do not universally convert every creative-video task into a slideshow.

## Scale, quality and layout
Keep physical proportion and editorial screen coverage separate. A product can dominate a separate showcase panel without claiming shared real-world scale with a hands panel. Never enlarge the object relative to hands to satisfy a screen-area request. Record whether coverage measures width, bounding rectangle or actual silhouette. Do not universalize a case's 57% width or half-canvas target.

Plan close framing before generation. Record source-native pixels, raw video return, crop, scale factor and final export separately. Enlarging a small crop or sharpening it does not recover native detail. Retain approved size, composition and print when fixing one defect; regenerate people/source only when needed and within the active scope.

Keep the complete original brand asset, smaller in information chapters and larger at the ending when requested. Do not replace it with retyped brand text. Put large, legible callouts in reserved whitespace and point inward without covering the product or printed message. Test label and arrowhead masks against the entire product/photo exclusion region, including transitions. Use 150 px headline and 300 px essential-copy previews; do not promise tiny source lettering or tagline readability.

Use warm restrained transitions that create clear changes of information. Check pauses/stutter, interpolation and loop joins. Do not confuse four editorial chapters with four independently generated scenes.

## QA and truthful receipt
Decode the full output; measure duration, frame count, dimensions, frame rate and audio. Inspect dense/all product-visible frames for print, edges, scale, hands, face crop, cable, surface texture, shadows, occlusions, logo and overlay intersection. Distinguish frame inspection, real-time playback and audio listening. Record technical, creative, fidelity and owner-acceptance states independently.

Case evidence (19 October 2026): a 15-second 1080-square dish film used a static hands strip and separate original product panel, with zero overlay-mask intersection across 360 frames; this did not prove physical calibration. A journal top-rim repair preserved approved size and had zero pixel changes below the narrow repair region before encoding; this did not certify every generated hand or unseen structure. Use these as bounded methods, not reusable product facts.

Video compositing permissions do not change the separately controlled complete-output one-pass thumbnail workflow. Read the active image skill for image production; never export a video frame and call it a compliant thumbnail by default.
