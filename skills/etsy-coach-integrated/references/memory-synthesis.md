## Durable principles to carry forward

### Buyer and product strategy

- Research first, design second.
- Start from buyer, recipient, occasion, emotion, and reason to buy.
- Use a repeatable research-to-listing loop rather than searching for one perfect niche.
- Layer a broad audience into a more specific intent when the product can honestly serve it: recipient + occasion, relationship + occasion, milestone + product, profession/interest + gift, or faith/use case + product.
- Evaluate a product for giftability, versatility across valid audiences, and potential for evergreen or seasonal variations.
- A personalized item can be differentiated by a name, date, year, Bible verse, family role, or message only when the product actually supports that option.
- Treat the journal as the product carrier; the buyer-facing promise should explain the person, moment, and emotional utility.

### MyGiftStyle product context

- Core documented product: personalized Christian leatherette journal / prayer journal / scripture journal.
- Documented finish: printed or UV-printed artwork on leatherette, not engraving.
- Common personalization: name, date, year, Bible verse, and family role.
- Historical shop observations suggested that personalized products performed better than many generic designs. Treat this as a shop hypothesis, not a universal rule.
- The user commonly creates UGC-style mockups and wants the uploaded product preserved exactly.

### Keyword and listing principles

- Do not use demand alone as the decision. Consider demand signal, competition signal, buyer intent, product fit, personalization fit, differentiation, and cannibalization together.
- Broad phrases such as generic gift, journal, faith journal, prayer journal, or Christian gift may be relevant support language, but their role must be decided from current evidence rather than a permanent blacklist.
- Keep a candidate's status visible: research-confirmed, data-aware, test keyword, high-competition caution, missing, or avoid.
- Do not claim a tag or title is validated when the value is only an idea, a stale export, or an estimate.
- Check every tag against the current Etsy limit and actual product truth before presenting it as usable.

### Images and conversion

- Do not rely on a row of near-identical hand-holding mockups.
- Use a varied image set that can show the hero product, personalization example, inside pages, material and specifications, how to personalize, use or occasion, and trust-building details.
- Product specification information should be visible enough that a buyer need not hunt through the description for size, page count, material, cover type, print method, or page style.
- Overlay text may clarify the buyer angle, but it must be readable, accurate, related to the listing meaning, and outside the original product design.

### Testing and operations

- Protect listings with meaningful sales or stable traffic; do not change a winner just to chase a new phrase.
- When a new angle represents a genuinely different buyer or occasion, a new listing may be appropriate; otherwise first consider a targeted edit or a new tag/description phrase.
- Check the shop for overlapping primary intents before creating a listing, to reduce internal cannibalization.
- Change a small, identifiable scope and record the before state so the result can be interpreted.
- Use Etsy's own Stats and search terms for post-publication learning; separate organic and ad evidence when possible.
- Keep all listing edits, publication, deactivation, advertising, and account actions behind the owner's explicit gate.

## Archive claims that need correction or re-checking

The archive contains fixed formulas and dated claims that should not be copied into the coach as universal truth:

- “≤70 characters,” “first 40 characters,” or a guaranteed primary-keyword-first ranking rule for titles;
- a guaranteed re-indexing delay or ranking loss after every title/tag edit;
- a guaranteed new-listing or renewal boost, or a claim that batch frequency is itself a ranking signal;
- fixed score weights such as search volume 30% and competition 35%;
- fixed thresholds such as “5+ visits,” “30–90 days,” or a mandatory 3–5 listing batch;
- claims about algorithm penalties, ROAS, costs, supplier facts, trademarks, or current Etsy features that are not rechecked from authoritative sources.

Retain these only as historical hypotheses or configurable local experiment conventions, with the source and date visible.
