# Emotional expression and mature gift-film typography

Read for gift-film copy, typography, mood review or VideoExpress repair. This implements the owner's 20 October 2026 feedback: adult commercial confidence and emotionally appropriate words, not merely a different font. It does not replace the source-lock or authorize publication, paid tools, new claims or altering the product's printing.

## Decide meaning before styling
1. Bind SKU, source revision, supported personalization, buyer, sender, recipient and occasion. Keep a missing relationship/occasion unknown; do not silently turn a general gratitude gift into a birthday, bereavement or Mother's Day product.
2. State one emotional job in plain language: e.g. thank a mentor for faithful support, celebrate shared years, express family appreciation, or celebrate Christ's birth. Identify which product detail makes the message credible.
3. Draft one short idea per chapter. Lead with a relationship-specific reason to give, support it with a verified product detail, then close with a restrained invitation/brand. Prefer concrete meaning over generic “Perfect gift”, “So special”, “Feel the love”, hype or repeated “warm” language. Do not invent what the recipient said, felt or experienced.
4. Separate three text layers: immutable printed product wording; new editorial overlay copy (draft/review status); verified specification/personalization statements. Changing overlay words never authorizes rewriting a verse, name, brand logo or cover design. Original blessings are not scripture.
5. Choose the expression as a coordinated system: vocabulary, type shape/weight, line breaks, hierarchy, contrast, whitespace, scene, pacing and transition. Review actual rendered frames and playback, not a font name or a claim that “serif means premium”.

## Emotion is not one universal warm style
- Gratitude: specific recognition, sincere and unforced. Calm confident headline, natural phrase rhythm, unhurried reveal. Avoid exaggerated praise or babyish pet names.
- Respect/mentorship: dignified acknowledgement and clear hierarchy; stable composition, generous spacing, restrained movement. Do not imply the recipient cured or rescued someone.
- Romantic commitment: shared time and chosen companionship, intimate but mature. An elegant display face may carry the headline, with a highly legible supporting face. No invented anniversary date or unsupported personal history.
- Christian Christmas joy: welcoming, hopeful, celebratory and explicitly relevant to the Nativity where the artwork supports it. Restrained festive movement/color, not mournful remembrance or childish holiday lettering by default.
- Remembrance: only when the brief/source establishes it. Quiet, compassionate language and slower transitions; no forced cheer, assumed death, invented testimony or promise of healing.
- Family appreciation: belonging and care without assuming hardship, a particular family structure or a religious recipient simply because the buyer is Christian.

## Typography and information choreography
Use a small coherent palette of licensed/available typefaces. A mature editorial serif with a restrained humanist sans is an option, not a compulsory pair. Avoid default-looking monospaced/system styling, bubble/rounded children's lettering, novelty effects and excessive all-caps when they conflict with this adult gift brief. A plain sans can pass when its actual composition communicates confidence and feeling. Do not replace the original logo with a typed imitation.

Create a clear headline/support hierarchy with deliberate line breaks and comfortable negative space. Avoid tiny decorative scripts, ultra-thin strokes and stretched letters. Set copy outside the product/photo exclusion mask; arrows point to the relevant detail without obscuring its print. Keep source-supported physical scale, people and the product unchanged while improving editorial copy.

Alternate upper/lower text placement only where safe whitespace and reading order support it. A modest fade or short slide can signal a new idea; movement must not make the message jittery or distract from giving. Do not move text merely to fill fifteen seconds. Scene, wording and transitions should tell complementary parts of one story. Music, if requested, must be licensed and mood-matched; a silent listing version must still communicate the story without sound.

## Review gates and regression tests
Freeze a copy sheet with exact overlay, line breaks, per-chapter intent, source of every fact, draft/approved status, typefaces and license/availability evidence. Recheck current rights rather than assuming a font bundled in a prior file is transferable.

Render actual 150 px and 300 px previews: the primary headline must be readable at 150 px and essential support at 300 px. Inspect letterforms, contrast over the real scene, safe margins, line breaks and transition frames; a numeric font size alone is not a pass. If it fails, simplify or enlarge in free space, not over the product. Preserve source-native vs enlarged dimensions honestly.

Review full-speed playback for readable dwell time, perceptible but restrained chapter changes, no stutter, and emotional consistency. Record subjective creative verdict separately from technical/fidelity passes and owner acceptance. Retain the earlier version and identify which words, type/layout and timings changed. A revised skill or draft copy sheet is not a revised or accepted video.

Test decisions before reuse:
- A Nativity family film with somber “Always in our hearts” copy: reject the unsupported remembrance interpretation, not just change the font.
- A mentor journal with elegant type but “Guided prayers for every day”: reject the unsupported interior claim even if the design looks premium.
- A name callout that is readable at full size but fails the small preview or overlaps the cover during transition: fail layout QA, preserve the printed design and fix editorial space.
- A remembrance brief with verified context: do not apply cheerful Christmas pacing or promise the gift heals grief.
