## Compose integrated typography and branding

Plan the photograph and text together before generation. Reserve real quiet negative space with plausible light and texture, outside the product and away from faces, fingers and strong edges. Do not generate a cluttered photo then force copy into a white card, opaque box, dark gradient or oversized footer as a repair. Intentional concise instruction layouts/callouts can pass when integrated into the reference-quality scene and useful for that role; this is not a universal ban on information containers. Ask for the intended quiet area in the scene prompt. Keep one clear hierarchy: primary buyer-intent headline, optional essential support, product, original logo. Product wording stays on the product unchanged; overlays do not replace a damaged name or message.

For the current reference-matched direction, use a strong upright serif whose actual scale, contrast and strokes survive the reduced-size render. Medium/semibold/bold can work, but the latest references also demonstrate large regular serif successfully; do not impose universal boldness or claim the exact reference font without verification. Use open counters, readable strokes and restrained line spacing. Other font directions require the applicable owner-approved brief. “Moderate” must not become the rejected hairline/weak-italic treatment. Avoid both heavy generic Arial-style headline treatment and overcorrection into hairline/delicate serif that disappears at 150 px. A serif label alone does not guarantee the reference feel. For original product-art editing verify actual font file/native availability. For generated thumbnail typography, specify the required visual traits and compare actual glyphs; do not claim a native font or exact family merely because the prompt names it, and do not rebuild the text in Canva. Product-name typography is locked separately and is never restyled to match overlay typography.

Use short, specific copy and intentional line breaks. Start with one or two headline lines and only the support needed for that function; adapt to the actual frame. Use warm dark-brown or another source-compatible color against genuinely light space, with strong local contrast across every letter. Test the real crop at 150 and 300 px: primary buyer intent must be readable at 150; essential instructions, units, field limits and option distinctions at 300. If it fails, shorten or redistribute copy, increase effective size/weight or change scene space. Do not call zoomed full-size readability a thumbnail pass. There is no universal pixel font-size or contrast number that substitutes for the actual test. Avoid copying historic “normal serif” advice as a fixed font mandate.

Use the exact supplied high-resolution original MG A5 identity, including its original geometry, spelling and colors. The visually verified current lockup is `mygiftstyle-a5-horizontal-transparent-1800x500.png`, 1800 × 500 RGBA, SHA-256 `d0bda79825a315e14e68722ab13b1f963828330f27f26261f4b64626d305da98`. Older open-book logos are different assets; a filename containing MyGiftStyle is not proof of the correct mark. For the current authorized pilot and subsequent roles, design a quiet light bottom-right brand zone upfront. If it is unavailable, recompose rather than shrinking or moving the mark as a shortcut. Verify original provenance, measured pixels and full mark before supplying this asset through Studio's current authorized generation route. Generate its proportional, faithful placement with the complete scene/text output; do not later place it in Canva. Do not accept redesigned, retyped, recolored, stretched or invented branding. A source reference cannot guarantee the generator preserves every pixel, so compare the actual output to the original and HOLD a failure after the eligible bounded retry. Keep it outside the product. Choose a clear contrasting scene region and a sufficiently readable size; do not blindly paste a tiny dark mark onto a dark wooden table or adopt bottom-right placement when that corner is busy. Preserve comfortable margins and do not cover a ribbon, hand or cover. Judge the real rendered brand identity at 300 px and recognizability at 150 px; do not promise that every tiny descriptor will read. If the original tagline cannot be made readable without overwhelming the frame, report that limitation and ask before altering the asset; never edit its lettering or quietly substitute a simplified logo. Verify actual final pixels, not merely an imported asset list.

## Lock the sellable product

Before judging artwork/style, apply [product geometry lock](product-geometry-lock.md) to each actual file: verified physical width/height ratio and source, explicit near-front angle, native front-face corner measurements/tolerance or a documented perspective HOLD. Canvas ratio, rotated bounding boxes and forced homography are not physical-ratio verification. Geometry SCREEN_PASS does not replace any visual/source gate below.

Before prompting, inspect full cover and detail crops of the exact approved source, including fixed title/body, artwork, name spelling/case, font shapes, baseline/spacing, placement, color, proportions, grain, edges and visible accessories. Record vulnerable details in the prompt. Compare final and source side by side at full view and close-up. Match the product’s on-screen display scale before judging grain: raw 100% crops from differently sized product images are not a fair texture comparison.

Reject material drift: fine smooth vegan-leather source cannot become heavily cracked, pebbled, coarse-grained, suede, wood or metal. Lighting may change appearance naturally, but cannot justify invented embossing, engraving, foil, raised letters or rewritten texture. Flat printed ink stays flat. Reject altered cover motifs, missing lines, invented lettering, a different name font, misspelling, name placement changes, distorted proportions, duplicated ribbons or unsupported elastic. A “close enough” design or disclaimer about drift does not pass. Simplify angle, occlusion, lighting or action and regenerate within authorized scope; never preserve a weak result solely because it is attractive.

Inspect complete artwork, not only the largest motif. UGC informality means natural surroundings and camera feel, not poor fidelity, unreadable product or sloppy rendering. If exact visual fidelity cannot be achieved with the available method, keep the result a rejected/held candidate and identify the blocker. Under the current whole-output instruction, do not switch to Canva overlays, cutouts or source-pixel compositing, including by reviving older permissions; apply the bounded built-in retry/HOLD rule.

## Check hands, anatomy and physical integration

Prefer the simplest credible action that serves the role. Before generation, specify number of people, number of visible hands, where each arm enters, which person owns each hand, whether it is their anatomical left or right hand, and each hand's job. Screen-left does not mean a person's left hand. Avoid ambiguous multi-person tangles, hidden unsupported palms or unnecessary fingers gripping printed artwork.

Inspect each visible hand separately:
1. Trace forearm to wrist, palm and fingers; identify its owner. Check left/right orientation from the palm/back surface and thumb side, not screen position alone.
2. Count only anatomically plausible visible fingers, accounting for real occlusion; do not demand all five be visible. Reject extra/missing fused digits, duplicated thumbs, broken wrists, reversed palms and impossible joints.
3. Check grip/contact: thumb and fingers wrap opposite sides plausibly; they do not tunnel through cover or float without contact. Product size must fit the hands. Nails and knuckles should agree with the visible surface.
4. Check occlusion order at every boundary: foreground fingers cover the appropriate edge, rear fingers disappear behind it, and neither hand abruptly cuts through the product. Protect important cover text/name.
5. Check ownership and action across the frame: handoff clearly belongs to two people, or a held journal clearly to one person. The receiving and giving hands must reach from coherent positions without impossible crossed limbs.
6. Check contact shadows, direction of light, perspective and supported weight. The book, ribbon, page block, table and hands share a possible arrangement; ribbon cannot pass through the table or end in an unexplained crop.

Review at full composition and hand/wrist/contact crops. If ownership or handedness remains uncertain, mark HOLD and choose a clearer simple supported pose, a single-hand edge hold, or a source-supported flat lay when suitable. Do not pass an ambiguous hand merely because a finger count looks plausible, and do not invent unseen anatomy to explain a bad image.

## Rendered acceptance and correction

Make separate verdicts for facts/product fidelity, style/composition, typography/brand, anatomy/physical integration, role/function and file properties. Use PASS, PARTIAL, HOLD or REJECT with evidence and the smallest corrective step. Test the actual final file at full size, 300 px and 150 px. Keep exact-file owner acceptance separate. A viewer should identify the product and intended buyer/meaning quickly; practical details must answer the slot's actual question rather than decorative copy.

If rejected, retain its filename/hash, source versions, failure category and review date. Correct only the authorized scope, version the result and recheck all affected gates. After a repeatable failure, audit sibling files for the same defect. No batch expansion from a failed pilot and no “100% guaranteed” claim. Report what was inspected and which limitations remain, without turning known product/brand/anatomy defects into acceptable final deliverables.
